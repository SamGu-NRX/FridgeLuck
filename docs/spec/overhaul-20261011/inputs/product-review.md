# Adversarial product review (GPT-6 Astra, high), condensed by the lead

The review attacked `product-direction.md`, `design-direction.md`, the engineering plan and the mocks. This file keeps every finding and the change it asked for. `SPEC.md` records which were adopted. Cost and adoption figures below are the reviewer's illustrative scenarios, not measurements.

Verdict: keep the dinner-first direction; reject the trust promises. The draft turns incomplete evidence into certainty: an ingredient exists, so there is enough; someone cooked something, so they liked it; someone finished a cook, so it can be shared.

## Blockers

1. **Unsafe food-safety copy in the cook mock.** "Chicken's done when the thickest piece has no pink", the camera answer "Still pink in the middle. Two more minutes", and "one breast, 6 min not 8". Appearance does not establish safe internal temperature, and cooking time does not scale with piece count. Change: a binding food-safety content rule for recipes, generation, voice and notes; reviewed food-specific checks with thermometer temperatures; timers say when to check, not when food is safe; camera looks state what they cannot assess; Adapt separates scalable amounts from thickness, crowding and equipment; substitutions recheck every eater's allergens including compound ingredients; swaps describe the difference instead of promising "no change".
2. **The fit meter claims more than its inputs support.** Fit is set membership today (`RecipeRepository.swift:100-116`): two eggs satisfy a six-egg recipe, a teaspoon of oil satisfies frying, an old scan keeps eaten chicken present, assumed staples count as owned. Change: quantity-aware feasibility over a complete ingredient bill (summed repeated lines, optional garnish separate), unit and preparation compatibility, allocation from lots without double counting across ingredients, swaps or a multi-dinner plan, and explicit states: sufficient, short, quantity unknown, assumed, needs reconfirming. Per-item confirmation evidence. Until then, copy like "7 ingredients recorded; check eggs and oil", never "You have everything".
3. **Automatic sharing has no consent or abuse contract.** A note like "made this for Dad after treatment" or a photo with a child in it becomes shared by default; forwarded invites admit strangers; small groups still allow harassment and monitoring of eating. Change: cook completion saves locally; sharing is a separate previewed action (or an opt-in table default with a per-cook private override); table sharing and global statistics need separate consent; aggregates use structured fields only, no raw notes, photos, exact times, pantry, health or taste data; free text and photos need moderation, metadata stripping, retention, withdrawal and deletion rules; membership approval, invite revocation, block, report, removal; a named moderation operator, otherwise uploads stay off; decide age eligibility (adult-only sharing is defensible). LeChef's backend persistence of frames and text needs its own retention contract (`liveSessionStore.ts:242-290`).
4. **Recipe count and promotion rules bypass quality gates.** Extraction is not a redistribution right (141 of 166 bundled recipes cite BBC Good Food with no license evidence; the other 25 are not automatically clear). One good cook does not validate allergens, safety, arithmetic or reproducibility, and a cook may have silently fixed a generated recipe. Requiring timers and grams everywhere invites invented data. Change: a rights-cleared coverage gate instead of "1,500 at launch" (illustratively about 100 reviewed recipes for a pilot, 250-400 for a first release if coverage supports it); text, image, modification, attribution, caching and offline rights recorded separately; generated recipes stay private drafts and a 4+ rating only nominates the actually-cooked revision for review.

## Major

5. **Tonight treats rejection as an animation.** No path for an unknown kitchen (camera refused, recognition failed), an empty kitchen, a three-item kitchen, "nothing fits" (fewer than 6 of 10 does not imply two good swaps), or a third "Something else". The 21:30 switch to Tomorrow dismisses late eaters. Change: one leading recommendation as a default, not a verdict; explicit unknown / sparse / nothing-feasible paths; first-class manual entry; honest shopping needs; after repeated rejection offer a constraint choice (quicker, different main, less effort, pick from a list); rejections never become ingredient dislikes; search and saved recipes stay reachable; the user chooses tomorrow.
6. **Cooking for two is not arithmetic.** One vegetarian and one nut allergy on one phone become one taste. Change: a meal context (eaters, portions, union of hard restrictions, time, equipment); feedback attributed to the respondent or labelled household; choose single-owner pantry or synced household pantry explicitly; a Table never implies shared inventory; multi-dinner plans reserve quantities across the plan and account for leftovers.
7. **Repeated shelf scans invent groceries.** Scanning the same two lemons twice adds two more. Change: shelf scans reconcile observed stock; receipts or "new groceries" add; ambiguous repeats ask; retried confirmations are idempotent; recognition confidence is not quantity confidence; one clear write boundary for scan results.
8. **Five observations do not make a preference.** Lentils because they are cheap, no coriander because none was bought, noodles because the app kept showing noodles. At three cooks a week, week 1 has at most three cooks and month 3 about 39, with perhaps 13 ratings. Change: separate observed behaviour, inferred preference and explicit restrictions; weight explicit dislikes above frequency; record whether a swap was necessary or preferred; count distinct opportunities with denominators and recency rather than a universal five; log exposures and feasible alternatives; controlled variety compared against the rule baseline; "Don't ask again" differs from "This is false"; weak inferences expire; the user can reopen a question; allergies survive a taste reset.
9. **Tables have no invitation benefit and do not solve N=1.** Flatmates eating the same dinner produce one event, not three; friends with different kitchens rarely match. Change: keep private tables as an optional experiment; invitations carry a useful object readable without the app; restore a bounded **use-what-you-have challenge** that works alone (three ingredients or a reviewed low-budget pack, viable recipes, a private result, optionally invite a friend to the same constraint). This gives the student, food-bank and sustainability story a home without a public feed.
10. **The 10-count floor hides scarcity and ambiguity.** Nine contributions do not make the next person "first"; ten cooks may be one person; quoted text can still identify. 100 users at three cooks a week spread over 1,500 recipes is under one cook per recipe a month. Change: "Not enough shared notes yet"; distinct contributors, denominators and per-claim minimum support; structured feedback first; show approved practical notes before cooking; do not promise aggregate community value at launch.
11. **The five LeChef jobs need degraded modes and device acceptance.** Extractor noise masks "timer fifteen", denied notifications drop alerts, offline recognition may be missing, "show" means holding a phone near heat, and the mock's "ok" is outside the five-word vocabulary. Change: a reliable local core per job (plan by deterministic allocation, substitute from reviewed context-specific swaps, timers created and acknowledged only by the device, amounts rescale predictably with timing changes only under rules, notes saved locally with a visible receipt); deadlines persisted independently of Live; device tests for lock, suspension, termination, permissions, Focus, audio route changes, multiple timers and reconnects; touch equivalents for everything.
12. **"Voice is continuous and cheap" is unbudgeted.** 40 minutes a cook, 13 cooks a month is 520 connected minutes per active user; at a hypothetical $0.01-0.05 per minute that is $5-26 a month. Change: a measured cost budget per completed cook and per active user, benchmarked on realistic sessions; local commands separate from cloud conversation; limits defined before a user hits them mid-recipe.
13. **The cut list removes control along with clutter.** Change: cut the inspiration feed, keep search, filters and saved recipes; keep history and optional nutrition with honest coverage out of primary navigation; Apple Health stays optional and confirmed-only; reverse scan asks whether the food came from the tracked kitchen and defaults to no deduction; model leftovers with portions, dates and reheating; keep streak pressure cut; budget or ingredient challenges are the test instead.

## Outcomes that would show the overhaul worked

1. Decision-to-dinner success, including empty kitchens and third-rejection sessions in the denominator.
2. Truth of the fit promise: audited surprise shortages and correction burden; unsafe or allergy-incompatible suggestions are release blockers.
3. Incremental value of learning against explicit-profile-only and current-rule baselines among equally feasible candidates; randomized where practical.
4. Week-4 solo utility: share of activated households completing two or more app-assisted dinners, solo and table members reported separately.
5. Community-caused cooking: recipients who actually cook a shared recipe or challenge.

Guardrails: cost per completed cook, timer delivery, safety failures, privacy failures.

Claims to rewrite before they can be tested: "gets better every time you cook", "a scan you finish in ten seconds", "works at N=1, 3, 10,000", "one pick feels confident or bossy", "fewer than a quarter of cooks happen in a table".
